# Galley Trademark Policy

"Galley" — the name, the wordmark, and any associated logos — identifies the
Galley project and its official hosted offering. The AGPL-3.0 license covers the
**code**; it does not grant any rights to the **brand**. This policy does.

## What you may do without asking

- State truthfully that your software "is based on Galley", "is a fork of
  Galley", or "is compatible with Galley".
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, reviews, and academic work.
- Keep the name in unmodified copies of the source, including packaging for
  distribution, where it accurately identifies this project.

## What you may not do without written permission

- Ship a **modified** version (fork) under the name "Galley" or a confusingly
  similar name (e.g. "Galley Pro", "GalleyCloud", "MyGalley"), or under the
  Galley logo.
- Offer a hosted/commercial **service** under the name "Galley" or a confusingly
  similar name.
- Use the name or logo in a way that implies endorsement by, or affiliation
  with, the Galley project.
- Register domain names, social-media handles, or package names whose primary
  element is "Galley" for a Galley-derived product or service.

## Forks

Forks are welcome — that's the point of the AGPL. Rename them. A fork must pick
its own name and logo and remove Galley branding from user-facing surfaces
(app name, page titles, marketing copy). Keeping required copyright and license
notices in the source is expected and is not a trademark use.

## Note on Typst

Galley is built on the Typst compiler. "Typst" is a trademark of its respective
owners; nothing here grants any rights to it, and Galley itself follows the same
rule it asks of forks: it is not named "Typst" or "Typst-anything".
